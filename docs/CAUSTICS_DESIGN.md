# Caustics: guided light tracing, then photon gathering

Status: **2026-11-10. Phases 1 and 2 and emission guiding implemented** (§4–6) as **Render › Method › Caustics** › *Light tracing* and *Light tracing + photons*, each also *guided* (`CausticMode`), off by default: light paths from emitter triangles and the environment (sky, image and sun), photons at surfaces and in media, spectral photons at shared wavelengths. Proposed: arXiv 2403.03641's Gaussian-mixture guiding instead of the histograms, and ReSTIR reuse (phase 4). The **Ring boost** (`sample_specular_manifold_caustic`) is an artistic approximation for procedural scenes 0 and 5 and is unrelated. Implemented methods are cited in [REFERENCES.md](../REFERENCES.md) (`VEACH1997`, `PBRT2023` light tracing, SPPM grid and equal-area mapping, `KNAUS2011`, `JENSEN1998`; `HEDSTROM2025` and `HUANG2024` as related work); the others are added when they are implemented (AGENTS.md).

## 1. Problem

Caustics are light paths L S+ D: from an emitter, through glass or off polished metal, onto a diffuse surface. The path tracer cannot connect to the light from the diffuse vertex (the shadow ray is blocked by the glass), so only BSDF-sampled paths that pass through the glass and hit the emitter carry them. With a small emitter that is rare: in Cornell Box 2026 (a 12 mm emitter inside slatted fins, `CORNELLBOX2026`) the floor caustics under the glass block are faint grain at 2048 spp, against the author's 2,000,000-spp reference.

## 2. Literature (checked 2026-11-09) and choice

| Method | Bias | Handles | Fit here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Light tracing** (particle tracing to the camera; the t ≤ 1 techniques of bidirectional path tracing, Veach 1997) | unbiased | L S+ D E, any chain of glass and metal, any light | Simple GPU kernels, a per-pixel splat buffer, no blur. Cannot do caustics seen through glass or in mirrors (E S D S L). |
| **ReSTIR BDPT** (Hedstrom, Kettunen, Lin, Wyman, Li, ACM TOG 2025; full text read) | unbiased | BDPT with ReSTIR reuse and per-pixel caustic reservoirs; "around 50ms per frame" at 1080p on an RTX 4090 | Its caustics come from light tracing ("light tracing techniques (t ≤ 1), which are often most efficient for sampling effects such as caustics"). Its stated limits: S D S paths "are still challenging … future work could apply vertex merging"; "the total storage cost of our full unbiased algorithm is 8.2GB" at 1080p; offline, "in some scenes … standard BDPT converging faster". |
| **Progressive photon mapping** (Knaus and Zwicker, TOG 2011, memoryless; Hachisuka and Jensen 2009, SPPM) | biased, consistent | every caustic, including S D S | GPU hash grid; slight blur that shrinks with samples; the only family here that resolves caustics seen through glass. |
| **Online photon guiding with 3D Gaussians** (Huang, Tanaka, Komura, Kitamura, arXiv 2403.03641, 2024; venue unconfirmed) | as photon mapping | learns where light paths should leave each light so they reach caustic casters and visible receivers; GPU (2²⁰ photons per iteration, RTX 4070) | Not a different estimator: a guiding layer for the light paths of either method above. Beats histogram and vMF photon guiding in 7 of 8 scenes after its initialiser; needs warm-up and persistent state across frames. |
| **ReSTIR FG** (Kern, Brüll, Grosch, EGSR 2024) | biased (vertex merging) | photon final gathering with caustic reservoirs, real time | Falcor/DXR code; photon based, so it inherits merging's blur; ReSTIR BDPT calls it biased. |
| **Specular polynomials** (Fan et al., TOG 2024), **manifold path guiding** (Fan et al., 2023), **Bernstein bounds** (Fan et al., SIGGRAPH 2025), **SMS** (Zeltner et al. 2020), **MNEE** | (near-)unbiased | exact specular chains between two given points; outstanding for one smooth refractor and a point-like light | Per-chain solves over triangle tuples: costly for a bevelled glass block of many triangles, and the slatted fins between emitter and glass make seeds and visibility hard. Long chains are their stated limitation. |
| **Streamed photon mapping** (Guzek and Napieralski, 2016–2017; full text unavailable, abstracts only) | biased | caustics from concentrated photon streams added to one global photon map | Pre-GPU-era variant of classic photon mapping; superseded by the progressive methods above. |

**Choice.** No single published method is best on every axis for this renderer. The combination that is unbiased where it can be, handles every caustic, fits a 16 GB Mac and extends the existing integrator one step at a time is:

1. **Light tracing for caustics seen directly** (the floor under the glass, the ribbon's reflected caustic on the wall): unbiased and sharp, the technique ReSTIR BDPT relies on for caustics, without its 8 GB of reuse state.
2. **Photon gathering for caustics seen through glass or in mirrors**, the S D S paths light tracing cannot reach, with the memoryless progressive radius of Knaus and Zwicker, reusing the same light paths as photons.
3. **Emission guiding** for both, since in scenes like Cornell Box 2026 most light paths die on the fins: first an analytic aim at specular-class objects, then the learned 3D Gaussian guiding of arXiv 2403.03641.
4. **ReSTIR BDPT-style reuse** only if the interactive viewport needs it, later.

## 3. Shared rules

**Specular-class vertices.** Imported glass and metals are not delta here (OpenPBR roughness ≥ 0.03), so both sides use one predicate: delta materials (`DIELECTRIC`, `GLOSSY` < 0.02), or OpenPBR with transmission ≥ 0.5 or metalness ≥ 0.5 and roughness ≤ `CAUSTIC_ROUGHNESS` (0.1 to start). In Cornell Box 2026 the glass (0.025) is specular-class; the ceramic glaze (a dielectric) and the bronze fins (0.32) are diffuse-class.

**The split.** Light subpaths keep only L S+ D (at least one specular-class vertex, then the first diffuse-class vertex D, where they end) and, with photons, L S+ M (a scattering point in a medium after glass). At the camera path's matching vertex D (or M), the path tracer drops exactly that suffix: emission and environment light reached through one or more specular-class vertices, and NEE taken at those vertices (their NEE and BSDF hit are one MIS pair). Direct light at D, and anything that leaves D through a diffuse-class vertex, stays with the path tracer. This fixed split is unbiased for light tracing and needs no MIS between the two estimators. It applies in `shading_kernel`'s bounce loop and in `volume_path` (Cornell Box 2026 renders through `volume_path`, because its glass has an interior medium).

**Light subpaths** (`caustic_trace_kernel`, one thread per path, default 2²⁰ per sample). When the environment emits, a share of them start from it (all without emitter triangles; else in proportion to the light each source's paths have added to the image since accumulation restarted, between 10% and 90%, half at first; `caustic_source_share`): a direction toward it from the sun's cone (with `sample_direct_light`'s sun probability), the HDR image's luminance distribution or the uniform sphere for the procedural sky (`caustic_environment_direction`), a point on the disc of the scene's bounding sphere facing it, and L · πR² / p(ω), the flux through the disc (the disc's area over its uniform position density). The others start from an emitter triangle chosen by power, receiver-independently (power mode's CDF in `images.emitters`; the light tree's host power table gets a CDF copy); a point by the existing barycentric warp; a direction by cosine emission about the winding normal, or one-sample MIS with the guiding distribution (§5); spot cones through `emitter_triangle_shaping`; Lₑ from `emissions[slot]` or `imported_graph_emission`, spectral through `emitter_spectrum`. Specular-class vertices continue through `sample_bsdf`. Media multiply by transmittance before the first specular-class vertex and in absorbing media; after one, with photons, scattering media sample a free-flight distance as `volume_path` does (`medium_sample_distance`), and where the path scatters it ends as a volume photon (§6). Photons carry power, matched to the camera paths' convention at each refraction: `sample_bsdf`'s `DIELECTRIC` weight includes the radiance scaling (n_from / n_to)², which the light path removes, while Adobe OpenPBR's transmission weight includes none, so its camera paths carry basic radiance L / n² inside an object and the light path divides by (n_to / n_from)² to match. Both factors cancel on leaving the object and matter for receivers inside one, such as Water Caustic's walls under the water. Shading normals need Veach's adjoint correction. Random numbers from a PCG `Sampler` keyed by path index and `sampleIndex`.

**Spectral transport.** A light path's splat is converted to RGB with `spectrum_rgb` at its own four wavelengths, which is exact for light tracing. Photons need the camera path's throughput and the photon's power at the same wavelengths, so with photons each sample's light paths and pixels share `CAUSTIC_GROUPS` (four) sets of four wavelengths: the wavelength numbers u_g = frac(u₀ + g/4) with u₀ drawn per sample (`caustic_group_u`), 16 wavelengths per sample image-wide. Light path i takes group i mod 4; a pixel's group is a hash of the pixel that advances with each sample (`caustic_pixel_group`, `pixel_wavelength_numbers`), so every u stays uniform and the estimate unbiased in wavelength. Photons store their power in four lanes and are keyed by group in the grid (`caustic_cell`), so a gather meets only photons at its own wavelengths and divides by the group's paths (2¹⁸). Hero-wavelength paths (dispersion) carry zeros in the other lanes on either side, which multiply out correctly. The cost: a gather sees a quarter of the photons, and the colour noise of one sample is correlated within a group.

## 4. Phase 1: caustic light tracing (implemented)

`caustic_trace_kernel` (main.swift) traces `PathTracerRenderer.causticPaths` light paths (2²⁰) per sample before the shading pass; `shading_kernel` adds the pixel's splat and, with `volume_path`, leaves out the matching light (`causticChain`, `caustic`). The emitter choice uses a power CDF of its own (`MaterialLibrary.causticEmitterBuffer`), because the light tree replaces the power CDF in the shared emitter list. The light path's scattering budget matches the camera paths' (`scattering_limit` + 1: the last specular-class vertex's light comes from NEE). `tests/Fix_caustics.swift` checks the sum against long path-traced references.


At D, connect to the camera: sample the thin lens (pinhole when the aperture is 0), project D to a pixel, trace a visibility ray (passing index-matched boundaries, times scene-haze transmittance as `medium_shadow` does), and add β · f(D, ω_light → ω_camera) · Wₑ · G / N_L to a per-frame RGB splat buffer with atomic float adds (checked on the Apple M4 on 2026-11-09: 2²⁰ concurrent adds summed exactly). `shading_kernel` and `volume_path` add the pixel's splat to the sample through `path_add(..., indirect: true)` before the running average, so the indirect clamp, export and OIDN treat it like any other indirect light, and drop the excluded suffix at the primary hit only. Higher camera vertices keep everything (§3), so phase 1 changes nothing but the directly seen caustics.

Memory: the splat buffer (12 B per pixel, 25 MiB at 1080p) and nothing else; counted in `renderMemoryError`.

## 5. Emission guiding (implemented 2026-11-10)

The guided modes learn, per region of a 4 × 4 × 4 grid over the emitter triangles' bounds, a histogram over 64 × 64 equal-area cells of the sphere of directions (pbrt-v4's equal-area mapping, `PBRT2023` §3.8.3) of the directions light paths left the emitter in, each weighted by the luminance that path added to the image: its light-tracing splat, and its photon's share of each camera path's gather (photons carry their histogram cell). Both carry 1/p of the direction, so the histogram estimates where the caustics the camera sees leave each light, not where paths were sent; training on every receiver a path reached after glass instead also counted the paths that only crossed Cornell Box 2026's bulb onto its fins. `caustic_guide_build_kernel` turns the histograms into CDFs before each sample; `caustic_trace_kernel` then samples half its triangle paths from the region's CDF and half from cosine emission, with the mixture density p = ½ cos/π + ½ P(cell) · 4096 / 4π, so each sample stays unbiased (`tests/Fix_caustics.swift`, guided cases). The histograms accumulate over the samples since accumulation restarted (the first sample trains only), and one path or photon in 16 trains, weighted by 16. Environment paths are not guided.

This is a histogram form of photon emission guiding, not the 3D Gaussian mixture of arXiv 2403.03641 (`HUANG2024`), which models the same distribution with finer, continuous lobes. Measured (`tests/PERFORMANCE.md`, "Emission guiding"): on Glass of Water it lowers light tracing's noise 4.4× per sample, but the guided paths reach the glass and bounce through it where most unguided ones end after one ray on the walls, so a sample takes 3.6 times as long with the same 2²⁰ paths (98 s against 27 s for 128 samples); on Cornell Box 2026 the slats between its emitter and the glass are finer than the 3.5° cells and the noise is unchanged. Guiding is therefore a choice (*guided*), off in *Automatic*. A path budget that shrinks as guiding succeeds, or the Gaussian mixture's finer lobes, would be needed to win at equal time.

## 6. Phase 2: photon gathering for caustics seen through glass

Bitterli's Water Caustic (`BITTERLI2016`) shows why this phase matters: its water is a closed volume whose flat front face stands between the camera and the box, so the walls, floor and boxes under the surface are all seen through it (E S D S L). Phase 1 adds the caustics the water surface reflects onto the ceiling and leaves the rest unchanged (identical band means with light tracing on and off, 2026-11-09); path tracing alone leaves that region nearly black at a minute per image, and the scene's own Mitsuba file renders it with `sppm`.

**Implemented (2026-11-09).** With *Light tracing + photons* (`CausticMode.photons`, `Uniforms.indirectReuse` bit 10), `caustic_trace_kernel` also stores each light path's end at its receiver, or where it scatters in a medium after glass, as a photon (`CausticPhoton`, 40 B: position, octahedral travel direction, power at its group's four wavelengths (three lanes in RGB), the number of non-delta specular-class vertices it crossed, and its emission-guide cell). The grid is a hash table of 2²⁰ cells of size 2rᵢ holding per-cell linked lists built with atomic exchanges in the same kernel, as pbrt-v3's SPPM grid is (`PBRT2023`), rather than the counting sort first planned: no extra kernels, no prefix sum. At each receiver after the primary hit, up to two vertices per pixel, whether the camera path reached it through specular-class vertices (the floor seen through the glass block) or a diffuse bounce (caustic light reflected on to the walls), `caustic_gather` sums f |cos_s| / |cos_g| × power / (π r² paths) over the photons within rᵢ that lie within 0.1 rᵢ of the receiver's tangent plane, arrived on the camera's side and fit the camera paths' scattering budget with the path so far (prefix + max(photon's vertices, 1) ≤ limit); the camera path leaves out the light the photons carry, as at a primary receiver, and keeps path tracing from a third receiver on. The tangent-plane test keeps photons on a neighbouring surface (the floor at a wall's foot) out of a receiver's disc. rᵢ follows Knaus and Zwicker (`KNAUS2011`, Eq. 15–16, read 2026-11-09): r²ᵢ = r₁² Γ(i + α) / (Γ(1 + α) Γ(i) i) with α = 2/3 and r₁ four pixel footprints at the orbit distance (`PathTracerRenderer.causticRadius2`, `causticRadiusPixels`); each sample's estimate is independent, so the running average accumulates it unchanged and the result converges as the radius shrinks (biased, consistent). In a medium (`volume_path`), each scattering point is such a vertex too, the first along the camera ray included: `caustic_gather_volume` sums p(ω_photon, ω_o) × power / (σ_s · 4/3 π r³ · paths) over the volume photons within the radius (`JENSEN1998`'s volume photon map; the normalization follows from `medium_sample_distance`'s weights, T σ_s / p on both sides, so the camera throughput needs the phase-weighted in-scattered radiance), with r³ shrinking by Knaus and Zwicker's Eq. 20, (i + α)/(i + 1) per sample (`causticRadius2(dimensions: 3)`). Surface and volume photons, and each wavelength group, hash to separate lists. Memory: 44 B per light path (photons and links), the 4 MiB table and 80 B per pixel of requests, about 215 MiB at 2²⁰ paths and 1080p.

Adaptations: a camera path split into its wavelengths (`spectral_split`) neither gathers nor leaves light out. The camera paths record each gathering vertex (`CausticRequest`, 40 B: point, triangle or medium slot, direction, prefix and throughput) in one of two per-pixel slots held in thread memory and written once at the end of `shading_kernel`, and `caustic_gather_kernel` rebuilds the hit from its triangle (or the medium from its slot), gathers after the shading pass and adds the result to the running average; the receiver's BSDF is prepared once per gather. Gathering inside `shading_kernel`, or writing the requests to device memory in its bounce loop, crashed the Metal backend compiler (AGX, macOS 27) of the RGB or spectral library. Recording requests in `volume_path` crashes it too when the spectral library compiles with relaxed math (a function-scope `#pragma METAL fp math_mode(safe)` did not avoid it reliably), and safe math costs every spectral render 12–14% (`tests/PERFORMANCE.md`). So the spectral libraries compile `volume_path` without them (`VIBE_MEDIA_PHOTONS` = 0), and the first spectral frame that renders photons in a scene with media compiles a safe-math spectral `shading_kernel` with them in the background (`requestMediaPhotonKernel`); until it is ready such frames keep light tracing, which stays consistent because they neither gather nor leave out. The RGB libraries record them directly.

`tests/Fix_caustics.swift` checks against path tracing, in RGB and spectral transport: the floor seen through a clear glass slab (0.973 and 0.974, 2 standard errors), the back wall it lights after a diffuse bounce (1.001 and 1.002), a floor inside a clear block seen through its top (0.83 and 0.84, 1.4 standard errors of a noisy reference; 1.74 without the refraction factor of §3), the floor seen through an absorbing slab (`volume_path`; 0.992 and 1.005), the slab in haze (volume photons; 0.995 and 0.994 over the image, leaving out one pixel where both renders share a rare camera path through the slab's glossy top), and the slab under a sun (environment light paths; 1.001 and 1.002).

Results: on Water Caustic (512 × 512, Standard MIS) the photons render the caustic web on the walls and the boxes under the water, and its interreflection, in 68 s, where path tracing alone and light tracing leave that region nearly black (62 s and 69 s). At 1,024 spp the mean luminance of each tenth of the image height is 0.89 to 1.10 times that of Bitterli's Tungsten EXR (0.99 on average, leaving out the top tenth, which holds the lamp; `tests/PERFORMANCE.md`). On Cornell Box 2026's front view, photons through the glass block's absorbing interior (§6, safe-math kernel) render the block and the ribbon's top with less sparkle than light tracing alone, for 209 s against 168 s at 512 spp and 960 × 540.

Not done: photon gathering in ReSTIR modes (phase 4); a third gathering vertex per pixel (later ones stay path traced); per-light guiding of the environment's paths.

## 7. Integration

- **Setting:** **Render › Method › Caustics: Automatic / Light tracing / Light tracing, guided / Light tracing + photons / Light tracing + photons, guided / Off**, saved as `ProjectDocument.caustics: UInt32?` (`CausticMode` 0–5), copied by `startExport`, `ShowcaseShot.caustics`, test override `VIBE_CAUSTICS` (`off`, `light`, `photons`). Automatic is Off until measured.
- **Strategy:** with caustics on, imported scenes render with Standard MIS, as scenes with media already do (`activeSamplingMode`); ReSTIR PT would need the exclusion in `pt_generate` and `pt_shift` (phase 4).
- **Kernels:** a `KernelGroup.caustics` (trace, gather and guide build), compiled in the background like the ReSTIR PT group and added to the spectral name list; mesh libraries only. The spectral media-photon `shading_kernel` compiles on demand (§6).
- **Parameters:** `Uniforms.indirectReuse` bits 9 (caustics) and 10 (photons); the light-path count, pixel area, radii, grid and guide state in `CausticParams`, passed with `setBytes`.

## 8. Validation

- **No double counting:** a small emitter over a glass slab and a mirror plate above a diffuse floor; with caustics on, the image matches a long path-traced reference (caustics off, many samples) within a few standard errors everywhere, in RGB and spectral transport. Light tracing is unbiased, so phase 1 needs no bias allowance.
- **Splat geometry:** a light path's splat lands in the pixel the camera ray through that point hits, with and without aperture.
- **Guiding:** the guided cases agree with path tracing as the unguided ones do; noise against time in `tests/PERFORMANCE.md`.
- **Phase 2:** caustics seen through glass, after a diffuse bounce, inside an object, through an absorbing medium, in haze and under a sun against the path-traced reference within noise plus 3% for the radius bias (§6).
- **Equal time:** Cornell Box 2026 `camera_front` and `camera_caustics` against a long path-traced reference; the default becomes automatic only if the caustic region's error falls at equal time and other scenes do not slow down, recorded in `tests/PERFORMANCE.md`.
