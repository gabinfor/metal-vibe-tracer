# Mitsuba scene import and Bitterli's scenes as downloads

Status: **2026-11-12: implemented and accepted.** The importer (§3–§5) opens Mitsuba scenes through **Open Scene File…**; the ten download entries (§6) open through it, with thumbnails and specs; the validation against Mitsuba 3.9.1 (§7) is done: all ten scenes are accepted under the user's acceptance rule of 2026-11-12 (§2), eight directly and two (Modern Hall, Veach Bidir) as differences explained by renderer changes since 2026-10-21. The development converter it replaces, `scripts/mitsuba_to_usd.py`, was removed on 2026-11-12 (§7). Design approved in conversation on 2026-11-10. Depends on the scene browser ([SCENE_BROWSER_DESIGN.md](SCENE_BROWSER_DESIGN.md)) for the download entries (§6); §3–5 do not depend on it.

## 1. Problem

Bitterli's rendering resources (`BITTERLI2016`) are Mitsuba XML scenes. Before this work the app could not read them: ten had been converted by `scripts/mitsuba_to_usd.py`, a development tool that needs Mitsuba's Python package, and compared with Mitsuba by `scripts/compare_scene.py`, but a user of the app could open none of them, and the download examples offered only OBJ versions of two of the rooms.

## 2. Goal and decisions

Decisions (user, 2026-11-10):

- The app **imports any Mitsuba scene file** natively, in Swift, with no helper process and no new dependency.
- The first version offers **the ten validated scenes** as downloads: Veach Bidir Room, Veach Ajar, Glass of Water, Country Kitchen, Contemporary Bathroom, The White Room, The Grey & White Room, Modern Hall, Water Caustic, Little Lamp.
- Archives download from Bitterli's site when the user clicks **Download** in the scene's details pane, which states the size and source beforehand; nothing is redistributed.

Done meant, in the original design: each of the ten scenes opens from the browser with its own camera, lights and path depth, and each scene's mean radiance ratio to Mitsuba matches the ratio recorded for the converter's output on 2026-10-21 to within noise.

Acceptance rule (user decision, 2026-11-12, replacing that criterion): every remaining difference from Mitsuba is measured and explained. The ratios recorded on 2026-10-21 are kept as history; the ratios of 2026-11-12 and their measured causes (§7, `REFERENCES.md` `BITTERLI2016`) are the record, and the importer is accepted on that basis. The ten scenes therefore open with their own camera, lights and path depth and are compared with Mitsuba 3.9.1, with every difference measured and, where a cause was found, traced to it (§7). The scenes are not claimed to match their recorded 2026-10-21 ratios where they do not.

Out of scope, reported on import: scattering media, `point`, `spot` and `directional` emitters, hair and other curve shapes, the other 25 scenes on Bitterli's page.

## 3. Importer structure

`Sources/MitsubaImporter.swift` follows `GLTFImporter`: `MitsubaImporter.load(_:into:progress:)` builds a `USDImportSnapshot` with mesh sidecars and one OpenPBR MaterialX document per material, and `USDImporter.document` turns it into the project (`format: "Mitsuba"`). The BSDF mapping is `MitsubaImporter.material` in `Sources/MitsubaMaterials.swift`, which writes the documents with `GLTFImporter.Writer` and bakes checkerboards, nonlinear plastic textures and bump normal maps into the import folder; a bump map's normal map takes its UV stretch from the first mesh using the material.

The tables generated from Mitsuba are `Sources/MitsubaTables.swift` (§5). The tests are `tests/Fix_mitsuba.swift`, `tests/Fix_mitsuba-materials.swift` and `tests/Fix_mitsuba-camera.swift` (§8).

- **Detection:** an `.xml` file is opened by the Mitsuba importer. **Open Scene File…** offers `.xml` (`MitsubaImporter.fileExtensions` in `SceneFile.extensions`) and `StudioController.beginImportUSD` reads every `.xml` with `MitsubaImporter.load`, which fails with "is not a Mitsuba scene" when the root element is not `<scene>`; the document is kept.
- **Assembly:** one mesh asset and one node per shape (world space, identity node transform), named by the shape's id, else `<type><index>`; meshes are read in parallel. One material per BSDF id, area-emitter radiance and interior medium, shared by the shapes that have the same three. Zero-area triangles are dropped and counted in the report.
- **XML layer:** `<default>` values and `$name` substitution; `<ref>` ids, which are global, including BSDFs nested in another BSDF; `<include>`; `<transform>` with `matrix`, `translate`, `rotate`, `scale` and `lookat`.
- **Mitsuba 0.6:** a scene whose `version` is below 2 goes through a rename pass to Mitsuba 3 names, the table `MitsubaImporter.legacyNames`, which covers the names the nine `scene_v0.6.xml` files in Bitterli's archives use (including `filterType` and `wrapMode`); other camelCase names are left as written and reported as unsupported properties. A texture's `uscale`, `vscale`, `uoffset` and `voffset` become a `to_uv` scale followed by a translation (`Loader.uvTransform`), and a conductor's `extEta` (a number or an IOR name) divides its `eta` and `k` and is dropped when 1 (`Loader.exteriorIOR`; with a named conductor material, or an `eta` or `k` that is not one or three numbers, it stays and is reported as an unsupported property). BSDFs' `<srgb>` colours are linearized with `MitsubaImporter.srgbToLinear`, the one sRGB transfer function the importer uses for hex colours, `<srgb>` and image decoding. Water Caustic ships only in this form.
- **Camera:** the first `perspective` or `thinlens` sensor becomes the saved view "Mitsuba: camera" and the active camera. Mitsuba's camera looks along +z with x to the left, so `to_world` × diag(−1, 1, −1, 1) is the camera (eye: the origin, direction: `to_world`'s z, up: its y). The vertical field of view comes from `fov` and `fov_axis` (`x` by default; `smaller` and `larger` resolved against the film's aspect, `diagonal` over its diagonal) or, without `fov`, from `focal_length` (`50mm` by default, over 35 mm film's 43.27 mm diagonal), and the film's `width` and `height` (768 × 576 by default). A `thinlens`'s `aperture_radius` (up to 0.5) and `focus_distance` (10,000, Mitsuba's far clip, by default) are the lens. A scene without a supported sensor, or whose sensor's `to_world` is singular, not finite, or has a z or y axis that does not normalize to a unit vector (axes of very different lengths, such as scales of 1e-20 and 1e20), keeps the default camera, with a report line.
- **Depth and size:** the film's size (clamped to 16–8,192) sets `StudioOptions.outputWidth` and `outputHeight`, and the integrator's `max_depth` sets `StudioOptions.depth` to `max_depth` − 1, clamped to 1–64; −1 or no `max_depth` gives 64 (the rule of `scripts/compare_scene.py`). A `max_depth` that is not finite gives 64 with a report line; a large one is clamped to 1,000 before conversion and then to 64, with a report line. A wrapper integrator gives its inner one's depth; a `sppm` or other integrator than `path`, `volpath` and `volpathmis` contributes only its depth, with a report line.
- **Lighting:** an `envmap` is the environment (its file, `scale` as the intensity, and its turn about +y minus 90° as `environmentRotation`, the convention of a USD dome (`scripts/usd_bridge.py`), `REFERENCES.md` `OPENUSD`); a `to_world` that is not a turn about +y is reported. A `constant` emitter is a uniform 8 × 4 Radiance HDR image of its radiance. A scene without a supported emitter stays unlit (`USDImporter.document` with `inspectionSky` false), as Mitsuba renders it without them.
- **Limits on untrusted files:** the import runs on a worker thread with a 512 KB stack, so a file that exceeds a limit fails the import with an error that names it (`MitsubaImporter.error`), not a report line, and nothing is partly imported. Every file, the scene's and each included one, is first read by a streaming pass that must accept it before Foundation builds the tree: it fails on elements nested too deep, on any entity declaration in a DOCTYPE (Mitsuba scenes declare none, and nested entities expand exponentially) and on any XML error. The limits are `MitsubaImporter.maxElementDepth` (128 elements nested, counted from `<scene>` through includes and resolved references), `maxReferenceDepth` (64 definitions each referring to the next), `maxIncludeDepth` (15 files open at once, the scene's own included, Mitsuba 3.9.1's limit), `maxIncludes` (1,024 includes read for the scene in all, every inclusion of a file counted, so files that include others many times cannot multiply), `maxWrapperDepth` (16 BSDF wrappers (`twosided`, `mask`, `bumpmap`, `normalmap`) around one another; the error names the material), `maxSubstitutedLength` (1 MiB for an attribute value after `$variable` substitution, which stops nested `<default>`s that repeat a variable from growing exponentially) and `maxSubstitutedTotal` (64 MiB of variable values substituted in the scene and its includes together, which stops many uses of one long variable). Bitterli's ten scenes stay far below them. Mesh files are bounded too: a PLY or a serialized sub-mesh that declares more vertices or faces than `SceneLimits.triangles` (2^30) allows or than the file holds (a serialized sub-mesh: more than a quarter of the Mac's memory, or more than its compressed stream can inflate to) is an error, and so are running totals over the scene's meshes beyond `SceneLimits.triangles` triangles or beyond `meshMemoryBudget` (a quarter of the Mac's memory) of mesh arrays (positions, normals, texture coordinates and triangles, `Mesh.bytes`). The totals are checked as the meshes load in parallel (`loadMeshes`): once one is passed, no further mesh is started, so they overshoot by at most the meshes being read at that moment, one per worker.
- **Report:** lines about a shape name it by its id, else its type and index (`MitsubaImporter.label`). The import report lists once, with what was used instead, unsupported BSDF, texture, shape and emitter plugins; BSDF and texture properties the mapping does not read; other and additional sensors and the sensor settings the importer names (film crop, principal point offset, `near_clip`, an unknown `fov_axis`); an integrator other than the path tracers; media outside a shape's interior and exterior media; `shapegroup` and `instance`; a shape's `to_uv`; unknown elements; a sphere under a non-uniform `to_world` (an ellipsoid here, where Mitsuba keeps a sphere); a shape with a singular `to_world` (skipped) and a sensor with one (the default camera stays); an anisotropic roughness; an `envmap` `scale` that is not a number (0 used) or outside 0–100 (clamped); BSDFs that are one-sided in Mitsuba (§4); area emitters inside dielectrics, converted or partly enclosed (§4). Not reported, and ignored: the reconstruction filter (`rfilter`), the sampler and the film's pixel format.

## 4. What is read

| Mitsuba | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| `obj`, `ply`, `serialized` shapes | One mesh per shape, in world space (`MitsubaImporter.mesh`); `face_normals` gives flat normals, and a file without normals gets smooth ones, as in Mitsuba |
| `rectangle`, `disk`, `cube`, `sphere` | Tessellated meshes (a 64 × 32 sphere of the radius's magnitude, a 64-segment disk with planar uv, where Mitsuba's is polar); a mirroring transform reverses the winding so the front side stays the analytic normal's, which is the emitting side |
| `flip_normals`, shape `to_uv` | `flip_normals` reverses any shape's winding and negates its normals, so an emitter emits from the other side (with a dielectric whose ratio is below 1, the two reversals cancel); a shape's `to_uv` is reported and ignored |
| `diffuse` | `base_color`, `specular_weight` 0 |
| `plastic`, `roughplastic` | `base_color` = reflectance × internal-scattering ratio (§5), `specular_ior`, `specular_roughness` = √alpha; with `alpha_u` and `alpha_v` and no `alpha`, alpha is their geometric mean (0.1 for an absent one), reported when they differ; an IOR ratio below 1, here and on `principledthin`, which the app's IOR (1.01–2.5) cannot take, is rendered at its inverse with a report line (`MaterialBuilder.coatingIOR`): the reflectance at normal incidence is the same (0.04 for `int_ior` 1 and `ext_ior` 1.5), while Mitsuba's coating reflects totally beyond its critical angle and the inverse does not; the internal-scattering ratio (§5) is computed at Mitsuba's own IOR ratio |
| `conductor`, `roughconductor` | `base_metalness` 1, `base_color` = F0, `specular_color` = 82° edge tint, `specular_reflectance` as `specular_weight`; a grey textured `specular_reflectance` (every texel's channels within 2 of 255 levels, so a grey JPEG's chroma noise still counts) is a connected `specular_weight`, the red channel of the sRGB-decoded image (MaterialX leaves float images raw), while a coloured one scales F0 only and is reported, as is one that cannot be checked (not decodable, or above 64 megapixels), which takes the coloured path with a report line naming the reason; a named `material` given with `k` alone reports that `k` is ignored |
| `dielectric`, `roughdielectric` | `transmission_weight` 1, `specular_ior`, `transmission_color`; zero transmittance is a black reflector; an `int_ior` / `ext_ior` below 1 (Glass of Water's bubbles in ice), inside `twosided`, `mask`, `bumpmap` or `normalmap` wrappers or not, is imported as its inverse with the shape's facing reversed and a report line, since the app's IOR is at least 1.01 and each surface uses only its own relative IOR (`MitsubaImporter.leafBSDF`, `reversesFacing`); an interior medium on such a shape would then be on its outside, where the app cannot attach one, and is dropped with a report line; a shape with an area emitter is not reversed, since the app's emission is one-sided, and its glass is rendered index-matched (`specular_ior` 1) with a report line |
| `thindielectric`, `principledthin` | `geometry_thin_walled` (a `thindielectric` with a ratio below 1 keeps its facing and takes the inverse ratio, an approximation that is reported: Mitsuba 3.9.1's pane at 0.763 reflects totally from about 50°, at 1/0.763 it reflects 0.06 at 50° and 0.34 at 75°) |
| `twosided`, `mask`, `bumpmap`, `normalmap` | The inner BSDF; `geometry_opacity` (a bitmap opacity is the red channel of the sRGB-decoded image unless `raw`, as Mitsuba 3.9.1 reads it); a normal map |
| `bitmap`, `checkerboard` | Image nodes (sRGB unless `raw`), sampled as Mitsuba does at the `to_uv` scale (normal maps included); `to_uv` offsets and rotations are reported and ignored; a checkerboard is baked to PNG; a missing file is an error, and so is a source above 64 megapixels that would be baked (`MitsubaMaterials.bakeTexels`) |
| BSDFs without a `twosided` wrapper | Rendered two-sided: Mitsuba's one-sided BSDFs (`diffuse`, the plastics and conductors, with or without `mask`, `bumpmap` and `normalmap` around them; not the dielectrics or `principledthin`) are black seen from the back, and so is the default `diffuse` of a shape without a BSDF; the app's surfaces respond on both sides, so a back face that Mitsuba renders black is lit here; reported once per scene (`MitsubaImporter.isOneSided`, and a shape without a BSDF unless it has an area emitter, whose default is black from both sides; an adaptation, `REFERENCES.md` `MITSUBA3`) |
| `area` emitter on a shape | Emission on the shape's material; a shape with an emitter and no BSDF has a black surface. An emitter inside `dielectric` or `roughdielectric` shapes (Little Lamp's filament inside its glass bulb) has its radiance divided by η² (`int_ior` / `ext_ior` squared) of each, with a report line ("area emitter inside dielectric '…' …; radiance divided by …"). This is a conversion between conventions, not a correction of either renderer: Mitsuba's emitted radiance is radiance in the glass, which its dielectrics scale by 1/η² on the way out, while the app's camera paths carry basic radiance (radiance / n²) through OpenPBR transmission, so an emission value is what is seen outside (`REFERENCES.md` `MITSUBA3`, `ADOBEOPENPBR`). Containment (`MitsubaImporter.emitterEnclosures`): 256 rays from the emitter's surface, uniform by area and cosine-distributed on its emitting side, are tested against each such shape whose bounding box contains the emitter's, in Mitsuba's facing; a ray whose nearest hit on the shape leaves it through its inside counts. A shape that at least 90% of the rays leave encloses the emitter, so a mesh that is not watertight still counts (Little Lamp's bulb is open at its socket: 99% of the rays); one that 10–90% leave is reported and not converted; scenes needing more than 2²⁸ ray-triangle tests in all leave the remaining emitters unconverted, with a report line. The division is made once, at import: the emitter's material keeps the divided radiance if the glass is later edited, moved or deleted in the app, so after such an edit the emission no longer matches Mitsuba's; opening the edited file again converts it afresh |
| `envmap`, `constant` scene emitters | The environment, with its rotation about the vertical axis; other scene emitters are reported |
| `perspective`, `thinlens` sensor; film; integrator | The view "Mitsuba: camera", the output size and the path depth (§3) |
| `homogeneous` interior medium with zero albedo (0.6: `sigma_s` 0) behind a refracting BSDF | `transmission_color` exp(−`sigma_t` × `scale`) at `transmission_depth` 1; other media are reported and ignored |

The importer defines the mapping; `REFERENCES.md` (`MITSUBA3`, `BITTERLI2016`) documents it and how it was validated. It began as a port of `scripts/mitsuba_to_usd.py`, a development converter to USD that it replaced (removed on 2026-11-12), and differs from the converter deliberately in these ways: the `sphere` shape (Little Lamp's emitter) and the absorbing interior medium (Modern Hall's glass); the converged plastic ratio and Mitsuba's default conductor `none` (§5); the horizontal field of view from every `fov_axis`; `flip_normals`; a texture's `to_uv` scale sampled at Mitsuba's rows, normal maps included; report lines for inputs the converter dropped silently (properties the mapping does not read, a Beckmann distribution, non-finite or negative values, a dielectric's `specular_reflectance`); the three fixes found by the validation (a dielectric with an IOR ratio below 1, a grey textured conductor `specular_reflectance` and its colour management); and Mitsuba's own mesh readers. `ply` and `serialized` are not used by the ten scenes and are included because other Mitsuba scenes commonly use them.

## 5. Values taken from Mitsuba

| Value | In Swift |
| --- | --- |
| Named IORs (`bk7`, `water`) | `MitsubaTables.ior` (`Sources/MitsubaTables.swift`): the 23 names of Mitsuba 3.9.1's `ior.h` table, read through Mitsuba by `scripts/generate_mitsuba_tables.py` (run by hand under the Mitsuba development environment; its outputs are checked in), looked up in any case by `MitsubaImporter.ior` (`REFERENCES.md`, `MITSUBA3`) |
| Named conductors (`Cu`, `Au`) | `MitsubaTables.conductors`: F0 and edge tint of the 71 presets and `none`, written by the same script; `MitsubaImporter.conductor` follows Mitsuba's default, `none` (a perfect mirror), when a conductor gives no material, `eta` or `k` |
| Explicit conductor `eta`, `k` | The unpolarized conductor Fresnel formula (`fresnel_conductor`) at normal incidence and at μ = 1/7, `eta` 0 and `k` 1 when absent, as in Mitsuba |
| Plastic internal-scattering ratio | `MitsubaImporter.plasticRatio`, computed with the formulas and quadrature of Mitsuba's `plastic` and `roughplastic` (rough integrals cached per IOR, alpha and distribution); `tests/fixtures/mitsuba/plastic-ratios.json`, 126 ratios generated from Mitsuba at a grid of IOR, alpha, distribution, `nonlinear` and reflectance, checks it within 0.1%; the ratios are computed to convergence (a 32 × 32 sampling of Mitsuba overestimates them by up to 0.79%) |
| Nonlinear plastic textures | Baked per texel and channel into the import folder, from the ratio at 17 reflectances |
| Mesh loading | Readers in `MitsubaImporter` for the three formats (the app's OBJ reader splits by material and group, which Mitsuba's `obj` does not); welding and computed normals follow Mitsuba's plugins and agree with it on the Bitterli scenes' OBJ files (`REFERENCES.md`, `MITSUBA3`) |

## 7. Validation

- `scripts/compare_scene.py SCENE.xml [--reference V3.xml]` renders the file through the native import (a `"mitsuba"` shot through `scripts/render_showcase.py`, with the film size times `--scale` and the importer's path depth, checked against the reference's `max_depth`) and compares it with Mitsuba 3.9.1's `volpath` render of the same file, or of `--reference` when Mitsuba cannot load it (Water Caustic's 0.6 file, against the hand-written `scene_v3.xml`). A cached reference is reused only when its recorded settings match.
- `--metal-ms` renders a second reference whose GGX `roughconductor`s carry OpenPBR's metal multiple-scattering lobe (`scripts/compare_mitsuba.py`'s `openpbr_metal_ms`), which separates the rough-metal energy compensation from other differences; its cache is kept apart from the plain reference's. A cached reference records the scene file's SHA-256 and the Mitsuba version, and a `max_depth` of −1 is noted in the result, since the app stops at 64.
- `scripts/mitsuba_probes.py` writes flattened variants and variants with a textured property made constant (with links to the scene's files and the `.xml` files it includes) and probe plates for the same comparison.
- Results of 2026-11-12, at the settings recorded under `BITTERLI2016`, are in REFERENCES.md: Bitterli's ten scenes, imported natively, have mean radiance ratios to Mitsuba 3.9.1 between about 0.97 and 1.04; every difference is measured, and each is traced to a documented cause or, for Veach Bidir and Modern Hall, to renderer changes since October 2026 that were localized but not investigated further. The comparison found three importer defects, fixed (IOR ratios below 1, a grey textured conductor `specular_reflectance` and its colour management), and a difference of convention, now converted at import: an emitter inside glass (Little Lamp, from 1.055 to 0.999; §4). It measured every remaining difference: the rough-metal energy compensation, Mitsuba's ray offset through Country Kitchen's double-layer blind, the black-reflector dielectrics of 2026-11-05 (The White Room), the medium and the converged plastic ratio. Modern Hall (−1.2% since the 2026-10-21 renderer, localized to commit `125b557`) and Veach Bidir (+0.3 to +0.8%) differ from their 2026-10-21 ratios because the renderer changed since; the native import equalled the converter's output on the same build. Under the acceptance rule of §2 (the user's decision of 2026-11-12) all ten are accepted; the recorded 2026-10-21 ratios stay as history. The converter, `scripts/mitsuba_to_usd.py`, was then removed (2026-11-12); results dated before then were produced with it.

## 8. Tests

`tests/Fix_mitsuba.swift`, `tests/Fix_mitsuba-materials.swift` and `tests/Fix_mitsuba-camera.swift`, without the network or Mitsuba, cover: defaults, references and includes; the 0.6 renames; each transform operation; each BSDF mapping; the IOR and conductor tables and the plastic ratio against the fixture; winding of mirrored rectangles and disks; `obj`, `ply` and `serialized` readers on small fixtures; hostile inputs, each failing with an error or importing and never trapping (references 5,000 deep in both definition orders, elements 10,000 deep and nested entities followed by elements 3,000 deep, both read on a thread with the worker's 512 KB stack, nesting split across an include, includes 20 files deep against the limit of 15, includes fanning out to more than 1,024, nested `<default>`s expanding beyond 1 MiB, a 1 MB variable used often enough to pass 64 MiB in all, wrappers nested 20 deep, `max_depth` `1e30` and `inf`, a bump `scale` of 3e38, NaN texels in a baked nonlinear plastic texture and a bump map, a source above the bake size limit, PLY and serialized files declaring more than they hold); a 0.6 scene shaped like the archives' (`filterType`, `extEta`, `uoffset`, `sigmaS`, `sigmaA`) with no unsupported line; sphere radius and non-uniform scale, a singular `to_world`, a sensor `to_world` whose axes do not normalize (scales of 1e-20 and 1e20), anisotropic alpha, coating IOR ratios below 1 and the one-sided report line (also for a shape without a BSDF); area emitters inside a closed, a nested, an inverted (ratio below 1) and an open-topped dielectric divided by η², one partly enclosed reported and left, and emitters beside glass, in a `thindielectric` or without glass unchanged; the running triangle and byte totals of `loadMeshes`, with limits passed in that three cubes exceed; a synthetic scene end to end (camera, depth, emitter, environment, report). The catalog test covers the ten entries.
