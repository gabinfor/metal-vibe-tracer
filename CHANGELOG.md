# Changelog

Metal Vibe Tracer is in early development: releases are validated builds, not stable APIs or file formats. Every method below is cited, with its adaptations, in [REFERENCES.md](REFERENCES.md); measurements are in [tests/PERFORMANCE.md](tests/PERFORMANCE.md) and [docs/SCENE_STORAGE_DESIGN.md](docs/SCENE_STORAGE_DESIGN.md).

## 0.4.0 — 2026-11-13

Scenes from other renderers, and a place to find them: glTF 2.0 and native Mitsuba 3 and 0.6 import, a scene browser in its own Scenes window with a preview render of nearly every scene, and ten of Benedikt Bitterli's scenes as downloads, which render within 0.97–1.04 of Mitsuba 3.9.1's mean radiance with every difference traced. Caustic light tracing and photons render light focused through glass, water and mirrors. A main menu with Open Recent replaces the empty start screen. Texture mip levels keep an image's brightness at every size, so some textured scenes render slightly darker in bounce light than in 0.3.0 (Bitterli's Modern Hall by 3%). Validated on an Apple M4 (16 GB, 10-core GPU) with macOS 27: the GPU verification suite passes (216 checks).

### Interface

- **Main menu:** the main window opens on a main menu instead of a blank start screen. It shows the app's name and version, a **Start** column (**New Project**, **Open Project…**, **Open Scene File…**, **Browse Scenes…**, and **Restore Last Session** with its autosave date when one exists), up to eight **Recent projects** newest first with their folders and a **Clear** link, the shader-compile progress and an action queued until the shaders are ready (with **Cancel**), and **User Guide** (the guide on GitHub) and **Credits and licenses…**. It scrolls at the minimum window size.
- **Open Recent:** project files you open, save or save as are added to the system's recent-documents list, shown on the main menu and in **File › Open Recent** (with **Clear Menu**). Opening one asks before discarding unsaved changes like **Open Project…**; a file that has moved is removed from the list with a message in the status line, while one on an external or network volume that is not connected stays listed.
- **Scene browser:** one catalog and one browser replace the start screen's scene buttons, the inspector's **Scene** popup, the **File › Open Example Scene** submenu and the Spectral examples and Example scenes sections of the Project & Export page. The browser lives in its own **Scenes** window, which holds the scenes only, can be resized and remembers its frame. It opens only on request, from **Browse Scenes…** on the main menu, **File › Browse Scenes…** (⇧⌘O) or the inspector's **Browse…** button (which replaces the **Scene** popup and names the current scene), whether or not a scene is loaded, and a second request brings it to the front; its close button or Esc closes it. The render keeps running and autosave and the last session are untouched; opening a scene, project or file from it asks before discarding unsaved changes and closes the window. **Credits and Licenses…** and **Show Downloaded Scenes in Finder** are File menu items, and the inspector's **Inspector page** control is labelled. It lists every scene as a card (a 480 × 270 preview, triangles and format, and Included, Download *size* or Installed) with the filters **All**, **Built-in**, **Included**, **Downloads** and **Installed**, a search over titles, tags and credits, and a details pane (summary, specs, collection, credit, license and terms) with a larger preview and **Open** or **Download *size***. Scenes can be chosen while the shaders compile; **Download** stays disabled until they finish.
- **Downloads in the details pane:** the consent sheet and the progress sheet are gone. A download scene's details pane states the size, source host and folder above **Download**, then shows the progress bar, status line and **Cancel**; nothing is downloaded before the click, the download continues while you select other cards or close the Scenes window, a failure (a full disk, a digest mismatch) shows its error above **Download** again, and finished archives stay installed. A finished download opens the scene when the Scenes window is shown and that discards nothing; over a document with unsaved changes, or while the window is closed, it only selects the scene (**Open** shows) and says so in the status line. Installed downloads add **Show in Finder**. A catalog scene that fails to open (an installed scene's file was deleted or is damaged) leaves the document unchanged and shows its error above **Open** in the details pane, as well as in the status line, so the Scenes window cannot hide it.
- **Scene thumbnails:** every scene but Happy Buddha and Chinese Dragon ships a 480 × 270 preview render, bundled in `Examples/Thumbnails` and shown on its card and in the details pane (the two Stanford scans show a grey placeholder until their repository terms are confirmed for redistributed renders). `python3 scripts/render_showcase.py --thumbnails` renders them from the showcase shots at 960 × 540, not denoised, at most 256 spp unless a shot sets `thumbnailSamples` (the spectral examples 512 or 1,024, the Veach, Bidir Room 1,024); their credits are in `Examples/Thumbnails/README.md`. Cornell Fog's thumbnail shows the **Atmosphere: Fog preview**, and a square film (Veach, Bidir Room, Contemporary Bathroom, Water Caustic, Little Lamp and Modern Hall) fills the 16:9 thumbnail by keeping the camera's horizontal field of view, which crops the film's horizontal band.
- **One Open Scene File entry:** **Open Scene File…** (File menu and main menu) opens a USD, glTF, Mitsuba (`.xml`) or OBJ file as a new scene, replacing the separate **Import OBJ…** and **Open USD or glTF Scene…** entries; an OBJ opens as an untitled scene framed to the model. **Add Model…** on the Objects page appends an OBJ to the current scene; the Objects page has no other import buttons.
- **Six more download examples** from McGuire's archive: **Bedroom** (a photogrammetry scan, CC BY-SA 4.0), **Breakfast Room**, **Fireplace Room** and **Living Room** (CC BY 3.0; the last two with Benedikt Bitterli's reference cameras), and Stanford's **Happy Buddha** and **Chinese Dragon** (research and non-commercial use). Each is 6–34 MB, pinned by size and SHA-256 like the others, and the details pane states what its license asks of renders. Showcase shots accept `"rotation"` (degrees about X, Y, Z for the imported objects), which stands the Z-up Bedroom upright.
- **Ten Bitterli scenes as downloads:** the scene browser's new Bitterli collection (Benedikt Bitterli's Rendering Resources) lists **Veach, Bidir Room**, **Veach, Ajar**, **Glass of Water**, **Contemporary Bathroom**, **Water Caustic** and **Little Lamp** (CC0; credit appreciated, not required), and **Country Kitchen**, **The White Room**, **The Grey & White Room** and **Modern Hall** (CC BY 3.0), each 3.6–26.6 MB, pinned by size and SHA-256 like the McGuire scenes. Water Caustic opens with **Caustics** set to light tracing + photons. **Credits and Licenses…** names both archives and offers both websites.

- **Camera presets removed:** the Camera page's preset popup (Perspective, Front, Close-up, Overhead) suited few scenes. Each scene opens at its default view; orbit, pan, zoom and the numeric fields move the camera from there, and saved views keep custom ones.
- **MetalFX is off by default:** new projects show the raw accumulation; the Render page's **MetalFX denoiser (preview)** checkbox turns it on, and projects saved with it on keep it.

### Rendering

- **glTF 2.0 import** (`GLTF2`, `GLTFEXT`): **Open Scene File…** opens `.gltf` (embedded or external buffers) and `.glb` files as complete scenes, with their node hierarchy, shared meshes, cameras as saved views (**glTF:** name) and a directional light as the sun. Materials translate to OpenPBR MaterialX, including `KHR_materials_transmission`, `_volume` (absorption inside the object), `_ior`, `_specular`, `_clearcoat`, `_sheen`, `_anisotropy`, `_iridescence`, `_dispersion`, `_emissive_strength` and `_unlit`, alpha masks and `KHR_texture_transform`; what is approximated or ignored is in the import report. Jakub Boksanský's Cornell Box 2026 (`CORNELLBOX2026`, 364,214 triangles) opens in 0.4 s with every material compiled.
- **Mitsuba scene import** (`MITSUBA3`, `BITTERLI2016`; [docs/MITSUBA_IMPORT_DESIGN.md](docs/MITSUBA_IMPORT_DESIGN.md)): **Open Scene File…** opens Mitsuba 3 and 0.6 scene files (`.xml`) in the app itself, without Mitsuba: `obj`, `ply`, `serialized` and analytic shapes, the common BSDFs as OpenPBR MaterialX (with Mitsuba's own IORs, conductor presets and plastic internal scattering; `thindielectric` as thin-walled glass with Mitsuba's pane reflectance, `principledthin` as thin-walled translucency), area emitters (black surfaces when they have no BSDF, as in Mitsuba), `envmap` and `constant` environments, an absorbing glass interior, and the first `perspective` or `thinlens` sensor as the saved view **Mitsuba: camera**, which the scene opens with. The film sets the output size and the integrator's `max_depth` the path depth (`max_depth` − 1). Glass with an IOR ratio below 1 (the air bubbles in Glass of Water's ice), a grey textured metal reflectance (Veach Ajar's floor) and a `mask`'s bitmap opacity (sRGB unless `raw`) render as in Mitsuba. What is not read, such as point lights and scattering media, and what is approximated is listed in the import report: BSDFs that are black from the back in Mitsuba (and the default diffuse of a shape without a BSDF) render two-sided, an anisotropic `alpha_u` / `alpha_v` renders isotropic, a plastic or `principledthin` IOR ratio below 1 renders at its inverse (the same reflectance head-on), a coloured or undecodable textured metal reflectance scales only the base colour, and glass below 1 is index-matched on a shape that also emits (so the light keeps its side), loses its total internal reflection as a thin pane and drops an absorbing interior. Bitterli's ten scenes, imported natively, have mean radiance ratios to Mitsuba 3.9.1 between about 0.97 and 1.04; every difference is measured and traced to a documented cause (`BITTERLI2016`).
- **Mitsuba emitters inside glass:** an area emitter inside glass (Little Lamp's filament in its bulb, also when the glass is open at a small gap) has its radiance divided by the square of the glass's `int_ior` / `ext_ior` for each layer of glass its light leaves, counted with 256 test rays of which at least 90% must agree (glass with `flip_normals` around it brightens it instead, a hollow shell cancels out, and glass that only partly surrounds it leaves it unchanged, reported). This converts Mitsuba's radiance inside the glass to the app's radiance seen outside it, once at import, with a report line; editing the glass afterwards does not redo it. Little Lamp renders at 0.999 of Mitsuba's mean radiance (its bulb 1.006).
- **Mitsuba files are bounded:** a scene file that does not parse as XML, declares entities, nests elements, references, includes or BSDF wrappers beyond fixed limits (128, 64, 15 and 16), reads more than 1,024 includes, expands a `$variable` beyond 1 MiB in one value or 64 MiB in all, declares mesh data it does not hold, or whose meshes together pass 2^30 triangles or a quarter of the Mac's memory is refused with an error, and nothing is imported; a non-finite or huge `max_depth`, a huge bump `scale`, NaN texels and a sensor `to_world` whose axes do not normalize are handled without a crash.
- **Caustic light tracing** (`VEACH1997`, `PBRT2023`; [docs/CAUSTICS_DESIGN.md](docs/CAUSTICS_DESIGN.md)): **Render › Caustics › Light tracing** traces about a million paths per sample from an imported scene's emitters through glass and polished metal (roughness up to 0.1) and adds the caustics the camera sees directly; camera paths leave the same light out, so the render stays unbiased (checked against path tracing in RGB and spectral transport, with a thin lens and through absorbing glass). On Cornell Box 2026's caustics view the floor's error fell 1.6× at equal render time, for 45% more time per sample. It renders with Standard MIS and is off by default; caustics seen through glass or in mirrors need photons.
- **Caustic photons** (`KNAUS2011`, `JENSEN1998`, `PBRT2023`; [docs/CAUSTICS_DESIGN.md](docs/CAUSTICS_DESIGN.md) §6): **Render › Caustics › Light tracing + photons** also renders caustics seen through glass, water or a mirror, their interreflection, and light focused into fog. The light paths leave photons in a GPU hash grid, at surfaces and where they scatter in a medium, and camera paths gather them at up to two surfaces or medium points after the first, within a radius that shrinks with each sample (memoryless progressive photon mapping), so the image converges as it accumulates. Photons work in scenes with absorbing or scattering media, and in spectral transport they are gathered at their own wavelengths, which a sample's pixels and light paths share in four groups. Bitterli's Water Caustic, nearly black under the water with path tracing alone, shows its caustic web and its interreflection in about a minute at 512 × 512, and at 1,024 spp matches the brightness of Bitterli's reference to 0.99 on average. Checked against path tracing through clear and absorbing glass, after a diffuse bounce, on a floor inside a clear block, in haze and under a sun, in RGB and spectral transport.
- **Caustics from the sky and sun, and emission guiding** ([docs/CAUSTICS_DESIGN.md](docs/CAUSTICS_DESIGN.md) §3, §5): caustic light paths also start from the environment (sky, HDR image and sun), in a share that follows what each source contributes, so the sun's caustics through glass, which path tracing could not find, render too. **Light tracing, guided** and **Light tracing + photons, guided** learn which directions from each light lead to caustics the camera sees (`PBRT2023`'s equal-area mapping; related to `HUANG2024`) and aim half the light paths there, unbiased; on Glass of Water the caustics' noise fell 4.4× per sample for 3.6 times the time per sample, so guiding is a choice, not the default.
- **Absorbing glass is coloured once** (`ADOBEOPENPBR`, `OPENPBR`): a solid transmissive material with an interior medium (`transmission_depth` > 0) was tinted by `transmission_color` at every surface it crossed as well as absorbed inside, so glass blocks came out greener (or bluer, etc.) and darker than specified: a 1 m slab of attenuation 0.5 per metre passed 0.125 of the light instead of 0.5. As OpenPBR specifies, the colour is now the medium's absorption only; thin walls and glass without a medium are still tinted at the surface.
- **Rotated anisotropy** (`MATERIALX`, `GLTFEXT`): an OpenPBR `geometry_tangent` connected to the tangent rotated about the normal by a constant (MaterialX `gltf_pbr`'s `anisotropy_rotation`) turns the anisotropic highlight by that angle. It was rejected before.
- **Thin-walled translucency** (`OPENPBR` thin-walled mode, `ADOBEOPENPBR`): OpenPBR `subsurface_weight`, `subsurface_color` (constant or textured) and a constant `subsurface_scatter_anisotropy` on a `geometry_thin_walled` MaterialX or USD material now render as diffuse reflection and transmission through the upstream thin-wall lobe, so paper, curtains, blinds and leaves glow when lit from behind, in RGB and spectral transport, with Standard MIS and ReSTIR. Before, these inputs were rejected unless at their defaults; subsurface on a solid surface (volumetric scattering) is still rejected, with a message pointing to `geometry_thin_walled`. The generated Adobe shader leaves a thin wall's subsurface share out of the dielectric multiple-scattering lobe, which otherwise added up to 10% energy under rough specular layers (`scripts/prepare_shaders.py`). The primary-surface cache grows from 128 to 136 bytes per pixel.
- **Cut-out foliage no longer glows in bounce light:** a material with textured or partial `geometry_opacity` reads its images at full resolution, as its coverage is read. Before, light bouncing onto a cut-out surface read a coarse mipmap that averaged the leaf colour with the colour of the cut-out texels (white in many foliage maps), so plants lit their surroundings far too brightly: up to 44% too bright behind the plants of Bitterli's Grey & White Room (the Fireplace Room), which now agrees with Mitsuba 3 there (tests/PERFORMANCE.md). Scenes with alpha-cut foliage, such as Sponza, San Miguel and the Bistro, render with truer indirect light near their plants.
- **USD dome lights face the right way:** a textured `DomeLight` imports with its turn about the vertical axis as **Environment rotation, degrees**, in USD's orientation (the image centre faces +Z). Before, every dome sky was a quarter turn off and dome rotations were ignored, so skies and their sun patches pointed the wrong way.
- **MTL `Tf` is read as 1 − transmission**, as McGuire documents for 3DS Max and as his archive writes it (`MCGUIRE2012`): `Tf 1 1 1` is opaque, so the Breakfast and Fireplace Rooms (whose materials are almost all `illum 4`, `Tf 1`) are no longer glass, and the Bistro's windows, bottles and cases and San Miguel's glasses are clearer (`Tf` 0.1–0.5 transmitted 10–50%, now 50–90%). Files written to the specification's reading of `Tf` render with the complementary tint.
- **MTL reflection models and `-mm`** (`MTL2026`): `illum` 3, 5 and 8, and 4, 6 and 7 when `Tf` makes them opaque, with a `Ks` beyond a dielectric's reach render as metal of that strength, so the Fireplace Room's mirror mirrors, its mantel lamps are brass instead of black, and the Breakfast Room's chrome and gold paint reflect; the `-mm base gain` map option now scales colour and scalar maps instead of being ignored.
- **Textured dissolve is coverage** (`MTL2026`): an MTL material with a constant `d` below 1 and a `map_Kd` is glass only when its name says glass; otherwise it is partly opaque, so the Living Room's paper ceiling lampshade shows instead of vanishing as thin glass. Untextured dissolve and the Bistro's dirty panes stay glass.
- **Textures beside `Kd 0 0 0`** (`MTL2026`): a `map_Kd` whose `Kd` is black is used on its own, as exporters mean it, instead of being multiplied to black. The Fireplace Room's table, painting and plants, the Breakfast Room's artwork and floor tiles and the Living Room's large picture show their textures.
- **Light tree** (docs/LIGHT_TREE_DESIGN.md; `CONTY2018`, `PBRT2023`): imported emitter triangles are chosen through a light BVH by their importance at the shaded point instead of by power alone, for every strategy and in both light transports. **Render › Light sampling** offers Automatic (the light tree), Light tree and Power; the choice is saved in the project. Nodes are 32 bytes (pbrt-v4's compact light bounds), emitters that share their place and facing (one lamp's triangles) share a leaf and are chosen by power, and a scene whose emitters all form one leaf skips the tree. At equal time it lowered error by 60–87% in Lamp Spectra, 8–17% in the Bistro interior and 15% in the Tyndall Tank, and cost about 5% in San Miguel (tests/PERFORMANCE.md, "Light tree"). Imported scenes with emitters render with less noise but not bit-identically to 0.3.0; **Power** renders as before. A fifth bundled example, **Lantern Arcade** (416 lamps over polished stone), shows the difference: at equal time the tree halves the visible noise with ReSTIR and cuts it by two thirds with Standard MIS. `scripts/measure_light_sampling.py` repeats the comparisons; showcase shots accept `"lightSampling"`.

### Fixes

- **Texture mipmaps keep an image's brightness at any size** (`METALTEXTURES`): Metal's `generateMipmaps` made each mip level whose level above has an odd side with one bilinear tap per texel, which skips some texels and under-weights others, so the coarse levels of most images whose sides are not powers of two drifted further from the image at every level, and bounce rays read those levels: the 894 × 894 floor tiles of Bitterli's Modern Hall were up to 11% too bright at 6 × 6 texels and 41–86% at 1 × 1, which made the scene 2–3% brighter than Mitsuba (its floor 3.8–5.6%). The app makes every material image's levels itself: each is the exact area average of the level above, in linear light, rounded without drift, also near black, so Modern Hall matches Mitsuba within 0.1%, its floor included. Mip levels stored in a KTX file are kept. Decoding textures takes 5–15% longer (San Miguel's 1.4 s → 1.6 s).
- **Caustics and media with the ReSTIR strategy:** such frames render with Standard MIS, as the inspector says, but the shaders were still told the strategy was ReSTIR. With **Caustics** on in a scene without media, the image lost its direct and indirect light at the first hit; with media, the ReSTIR candidates were generated and discarded, and MetalFX could run on batched samples. MetalFX is now off for these frames (docs/VOLUME_DESIGN.md).
- **Inspection views keep a caustic render's accumulation:** switching to a guide view and back restarted the render from sample 1.
- **Narrow-band lights keep their brightness:** a light's own spectrum is evaluated at the 1 nm node its normalisation uses. Bands narrower than about 1.5 nm lit the scene 5% (1 nm) to several times (0.5 nm) off their intended luminance, depending on the band's centre.
- **Interior media with `transmission_scatter_anisotropy` of ±1** rendered black; the value is limited to ±0.99, as the scene medium's is.
- **Popups with repeated names:** two objects or materials of the same name (two unnamed OBJ objects, glTF defaults) showed as one entry in the **Scene object**, **Parent** and material popups, which then selected, reparented or assigned the wrong one.
- **One undo step per drag:** a colour-panel drag or scroll zoom longer than half a second recorded a step every half second.
- **Closing the window** asks to quit before the window goes, so cancelling after a failed save leaves the document open and savable.
- **glTF:** a file with more than 1,016 materials is refused with a message instead of crashing; a node with zero scale hides its subtree (and is reported) instead of failing the import; malformed accessors (negative offsets or strides, non-integer or negative indices, oversized counts) are rejected; `COLOR_0` vertex colours are reported as not imported; an anisotropy rotation beyond a full turn no longer drops the material. Files whose accessors share large buffer views, or with thousands of accessors, open faster.
- **Large scene graphs** validate in linear time (parents and meshes were searched per node), and the Objects page no longer decodes a selected mesh's triangles to count them.
- **Memory checks:** an OIDN export of a scene with media or caustics is budgeted for the Standard MIS frame it renders, not for ReSTIR reservoirs; a failed photon-buffer reallocation after a resize no longer leaves smaller buffers in use.
- **Spectral renders are reproducible across launches** with several blackbody or narrow-band lights (their order followed a dictionary's).
- A failed MaterialX import shows its own error instead of appending to the previous report; the Scene popup returns to the current scene when a choice is refused during an export; HDR exports are written once instead of twice; an OBJ's missing texture is reported once; the stochastic pairwise MIS passes reuse the selected sample's target instead of evaluating it again; the **Ring boost** no longer draws an unused light sample in the Cornell box (its noise pattern there changes; the mean does not).

## 0.3.0 — 2026-10-26

Light as a spectrum, in scenes made to show it: participating media (haze, milky and Rayleigh-scattering volumes), per-light spectra (blackbody, CIE presets, laser lines), spot and beam lights, and four spectral example scenes included with the app, rendered for the README without a denoiser. An optional indirect clamp removes fireflies at the cost of bias. Existing scenes render as before. Projects saved by 0.3.0 can hold media, light spectra and the clamp, which 0.2.x ignores. Validated on an Apple M4 (16 GB, 10-core GPU) with macOS 27: the GPU verification suite passes (198 checks).

### Rendering

- **Participating media** (docs/VOLUME_DESIGN.md): homogeneous scene haze (Lighting › Atmosphere › *Participating medium*, or `vibe:medium` attributes on a USD stage's default prim) and object interiors from OpenPBR `transmission_depth`, `transmission_scatter` and `transmission_scatter_anisotropy`, with index-matched boundaries (`specular_weight` 0), Henyey–Greenstein phase functions (`HG1941`) and an optional Rayleigh λ⁻⁴ law (`BUCHOLTZ1995`). Distances are sampled once for all wavelengths with a throughput-weighted mixture over them (`ADOBEOPENPBR`, `PBRT2023`). Scenes with media render with Standard MIS.
- **Per-light spectra**: each imported light can have a CIE preset, a blackbody temperature (`PLANCK`; UsdLux `colorTemperature`) or a narrow band such as a laser line, normalised to equal luminance and included in wavelength sampling; editable on the Materials page.
- **Spot and beam lights**: UsdLux `ShapingAPI` cones, softness and focus on rectangle and disk lights (`OPENUSD`), editable on the Materials page; shaped lights are selected in proportion to their cone's power.
- `Uniforms` grows from 304 to 336 bytes; per-slot material parameters from 3 to 8 float4.
- **Indirect clamp** (Render page; `StudioOptions.indirectClamp`, `"indirectClamp"` per showcase shot): an optional, biased per-sample cap on the luminance of indirect light (two or more scattering events; with unified ReSTIR PT its whole estimate), which removes fireflies. Off by default; when off, renders are bit-identical to before (`tests/Fix_clamp.swift`). `Uniforms.mediumOptions` is renamed `pathOptions`, whose w holds the cap.

### Scenes and import

- **Spectral example scenes**, included with the app: **Tyndall Tank** (Rayleigh scattering of a beam) and **Lamp Spectra** (five lamp spectra in haze), and **Newton's Colour Series** (thin-film interference: soap films 100–800 nm, anodized titanium 30–150 nm; `DIAMANTI2014`) and **Abbe Row** (a slit seen through 60° prisms of SCHOTT N-SF11, F2, N-BK7 and N-FK51A at minimum deviation; `SCHOTTGLASS`). They are OpenUSD stages in `Examples/Spectral`, listed first in File › Open Example Scene and on the File page, and open without a download or an import report.
- **OpenUSD implicit prims:** `Sphere`, `Cube` and `Cylinder` prims are tessellated from their schema attributes (smooth normals, outward winding) instead of being skipped (`OPENUSD`).
- `scripts/render_showcase.py` exports the raw accumulation unless a shot sets `"denoise": true`, and prints samples over the target and elapsed time every 30 s.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-10-23

121 commits since 0.1.0. Validated on an Apple M4 (16 GB, 10-core GPU) with macOS 27: the GPU verification suite passes (189 checks).

### Rendering

- **ReSTIR PT** with the ReSTIR PT Enhanced improvements (`RESTIRPT2022`, `RESTIRPTE2026`): whole-path reuse, including glossy, mirror and glass paths; the default for imported scenes.
- **Spatial and temporal reuse options:** compatibility-guided neighbour selection (`COMPATRESTIR2026`), stochastic pairwise MIS (`SPMIS2026`), multi-layer reservoir splatting (`HONG2026`, `LIU2025`) and spatio-temporal control variates for ReSTIR PT shading (`RESTCV2026`), each selectable on the Render page.
- **Spectral light transport**, now the default: four wavelengths per path, bounded-MESE reflectance spectra from sRGB, CIE illuminant presets for lights and the sun, dispersion and thin-film interference (`PETERS2019`, `FOURIERSRGB2019`, `PETERSBLOG2025`, `HERO2014`).
- **Z++ sampler** (`ZPP2026`), opt-in for every strategy.
- **Two-level acceleration** with instancing: Metal hardware ray tracing where a launch-time check finds it watertight, an exact software 4-wide BVH otherwise; batched builds and instance-structure refits.
- **Anisotropic ray-cone texture filtering** (`RAYCONES2021`) with pbrt-v4's sample-count footprint scale at primary hits (`PBRT2023`).
- **Watertight ray/triangle intersection**, error-bounded ray offsets, and corrected HDRI, sun, sphere and emitter light sampling.

### Materials

- **OpenPBR alpha cutouts** from `geometry_opacity` (stochastic coverage), with an opaque-instance fast path in hardware traversal.
- **Glass:** OpenPBR `transmission_color` and `geometry_thin_walled`; MTL glass (`illum` 4/6/7/9, or constant dissolve) as smooth, thin-walled or solid transmission.
- **MaterialX:** graph-driven emission, constant `specular_color`, images downscaled to fit the GPU texture budget, and fixes to rotate2d, image decoding and colour spaces.
- **Open Image Denoise guides** taken behind smooth glass, so glass no longer blurs what shows through it.

### Scenes and import

- **Large scenes:** indexed geometry (64 B per triangle with software traversal, 150 B with hardware, down from 160 and 246), 1,024 material slots, 4,096 graph images, 65,535 scene nodes; memory budgets and triangle limits scale with the Mac's GPU. San Miguel (10 M triangles, 287 materials) imports from OBJ in about 12 s.
- **OBJ/MTL import:** a streaming OBJ reader; MTL materials translated to OpenPBR MaterialX, including glass, coverage, emission and bump-to-normal conversion at a strength calibrated on authored normal maps.
- **OpenUSD:** binary mesh sidecars instead of JSON, PreviewSurface emission, UsdLux power and sun fixes, robust import.
- **Projects:** format 4 (indexed meshes, content-addressed assets, autosave sidecars); embedded images up to 1 GiB. Formats 1–3 open and are saved as format 4; 0.1.0 cannot open format-4 projects.

### Performance and scaling

- Samples per frame are sized from measured GPU time, the viewport follows the display's refresh rate, and exports keep two command buffers in flight, so faster GPUs are not held to one sample per frame.
- Cutout scenes keep opaque instances on the hardware fast path (Sponza 15–23% faster, Bistro about 10%).

### Validation

- **Mitsuba 3** comparisons (`MITSUBA3`, `BITTERLI2016`): the built-in Cornell box agrees within 0.02% of mean radiance and Veach MIS within 0.11%; five of seven Bitterli scenes agree within 2.5%, and the remaining gaps are documented model differences.
- The verification suite runs in the Swift 6 language mode through the production `renderFrame` path, with statistical tolerances and MetalFX energy, fallback and motion checks.

### Documentation

- README with production-scene renders (Sponza, San Miguel, Bistro), validation side-by-sides and before/after comparisons; CONTRIBUTING.md; the MIT license scoped to the project's own code, with third-party notices.

### Known limitations

- ReSTIR PT costs 1.3–2.3× the frame time, so procedural scenes default to first-bounce ReSTIR GI.
- Spectral MIS for rough dispersive glass and lane splitting are experimental and off by default.
- Glass makes paths continue into building interiors: a 960×540, 64-spp Bistro exterior export took 179 s with glass against 67 s with the former opaque panes. The cost of the new texture filtering against the old has not been measured in the same harness yet.
- Large texture sets are downscaled to fit the GPU budget; block compression is not implemented.
- OpenUSD import is a snapshot (no animation, subdivision, volumes or curves); MaterialX support is a bounded importer.
- The app is an unsigned, non-notarized build.
